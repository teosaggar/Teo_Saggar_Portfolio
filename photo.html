Observation

Photography accompanies the way I explore the world. More than a tool for representation, it is a way of observing, slowing down, and paying attention to what surrounds me. Most of my photographs are taken while traveling, walking, or exploring unfamiliar places. They preserve the trace of a moment, an encounter, an atmosphere, or a detail that might otherwise have gone unnoticed. Photographing becomes a way of looking more carefully: a particular light on a wall, vegetation reclaiming a ruin, a way of inhabiting a place, or a landscape encountered along the way. This practice also informs my work as an architecture student. It helps me observe the relationships between people, objects, and environments, and to better understand the uses, temporalities, and transformations that shape a territory. Each photograph is therefore both a memory and an attempt to understand the world through attentive observation.
