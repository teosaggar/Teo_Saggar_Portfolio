Les Formes de la Réhabilitation

The initial site consists of two distinct plots. The first plot is located at the intersection of two departmental roads and contains a 1970s cubic building housing a community center with a concrete outdoor space. The second plot consists of a private house built with flint stone, featuring a garden and an outbuilding. The two plots are separated by a street, with a flint wall marking the boundary of the house plot. For this project, my first intention was to unify these two plots by deconstructing the flint wall, pedestrianizing the street that separates them, and retaining only the ground floor structure of the community center. These interventions provide views throughout the site and facilitate circulation. To address the lack of public green spaces nearby, a large portion of the built ground was re-naturalized. The planting of trees across the plots helps to isolate the site from the surrounding roads. The program, proposed by the professor, was integrated into the existing structure as well as in new, punctual constructions designed to blend into their environment through curved forms. The architectural styles and materials used aim to make each space unique. The convex roofs of the covered walkway and café break the orthogonal geometry of the community center. The post-and-beam structure added to the community center, like a new skin, extends the first floor with an outdoor space. It also provides shade to an interior exposed to the sun from both the north and south.
