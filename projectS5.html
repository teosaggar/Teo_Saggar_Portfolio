Cicatrisation d'un Paysage Ruiné

On June 10, 2014, the terrorist organization Daesh seized the city of Mosul in northern Iraq after four days of figh ting against the military forces stationed there. Daesh maintained control over the city until 2017, having already caused widespread destruction, particularly by targeting spaces dedicated to culture, education, pre-Islamic civi lizations, and anything that contradicted their ideology. The “Battle of Mosul” took place between October 2016 and July 2017, opposing Daesh and the Iraqi army. On July 10, 2017, the Iraqi government officially declared the full “liberation” of Mosul, a liberation that cost the lives of thousands and left more than 50% of the city in ruins. In line with the studio’s pedagogy, the project began with an extended phase of research, through books, docu mentaries, and online investigations. This process al lowed me to absorb the history of the city, understand its geographical context and related constraints, but also to engage with more abstract notions such as the sym bolism of ruins and the concept of “negative memory.” Representations were gradually integrated throughout the semester, first to illustrate the issues at stake, and later to com municate the proposed interventions. Plaster, a key building material used in the traditional houses of Mosul’s Old City, became the central medium of representation for this project. The project developed for this studio consists in imagining a process of clearing the site, implementing subtle interventions on the ruins, and proposing a temporary program as a first step toward a long-term reconstruc tion carried out with the local inhabitants. The implementation process of these interven tions was represented through a plaster and stone model, designed to reflect the effects of time on the site. This temporal evolution was captured through a stop-motion video. Be low are photographs of three major phases in the site’s transformation: before destruc tion, after destruction, and after intervention. The intervention process begins with the re moval of rubble and the loosening of the soil to allow vegetation to return. It continues with primary actions for stabilizing and protecting the ruins: proposed techniques include stone replacement, repointing, installation of an chor rods, and protective lime mortar coatings. Finally, the rehabilitation phase aims to give the ruins new uses through the in corporation of local, low-tech mate rials such as cotton fabric or palm fronds. The ruin is reborn from its own rem nants, reactivated through the reuse of construction waste, repurposed as ga bions or bags filled with crushed debris. These added elements contribute to a sensi tive requalification of the devastated lands cape, while also providing shade and comfort. To conclude the narrative of this project, I would like to clarify the motivations that jus tify it, at least on the scale of a fictional project developed by a student in architecture school. The first challenge I encountered while addres sing post-war architecture was the meaning of designing a park in a city where many inhabi tants no longer even have a roof over their heads. This is why I sought to create a project for the people, a project that aims to contribute to the revitalization of a city that has been grieving for over a decade. One part of the program is intended to of fer a peaceful space to the residents by sup porting youth through sports facilities, study areas, playgrounds for young children, spaces for discussion and places for artistic expression. A program that stands in complete opposition to the ideology of Daesh, which forbade access to education, art, social interaction and leisure. A second part of the program aims to encourage the reconstruction of nearby housing by the re sidents themselves. A sorting center will be esta blished on site, operational from the beginning of the construction process and remaining active afterward. A training center for reconstruction will extend the participatory building experience and of fer a space for professional reintegration, particularly for young people from Mosul. It will simultaneously support the rebuilding of the city’s housing stock. In this way, this park is envisioned as a central space in both the psychological healing of the inha bitants and the material reconstruction of the city
